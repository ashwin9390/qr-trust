Issue a signed QR, try forging it, then verify it before anything opens. Everything runs in your browser, and keys exist only for this session.

Issue a signed QR

The issuer signs the destination, expiry and a one-time nonce with its private key.

Attack lab

Verify

Paste the QR text, upload a photo of a QR, or scan with the camera.

Threat model

How attackers use QR codes, and what this PoC does about each.

Fake login and payment pages (quishing)

A QR leads to a cloned sign-in, MFA prompt or checkout. Often pasted into emails as an image so text filters never see the link.

PoC: unsigned codes never get TRUSTED, and lookalike domains are flagged. Gap: a convincing lookalike with ASCII characters can still score low.

Sticker swaps in public places

A fake code is stuck over a real one on a parking meter, menu, poster or ATM, so victims pay or sign in on the attacker’s site.

PoC: the real signed code verifies, the sticker does not. Gap: only helps if people scan with a verifying scanner, not the stock camera.

Auto-action payloads

QR content is not always a web link. It can trigger a call, SMS, Wi-Fi join, contact import, app install or payment request, so one scan sets up the next step.

PoC: only https URLs can pass, other schemes are blocked. Gap: Wi-Fi and payment formats are not parsed yet.

Login-by-QR hijacking

Services that sign you in by scanning a QR can be abused by showing the victim the attacker’s live login QR, so the victim approves the attacker’s session.

PoC: single-use, short-expiry nonces limit this. Gap: needs binding to the user’s own session and device.

Hiding from scanners

Split or nested codes, QR drawn from text characters, and redirect chains that show a clean page to security tools but a malicious one to real visitors.

PoC: unsigned codes are never trusted regardless of what the page shows. Gap: no sandbox or click-time re-check yet.

Tracking the scanner

The landing page can fingerprint the device (model, battery, location), profile the victim, or tell targets apart from analysts.

PoC: the verifier never opens the destination. Gap: the page that finally opens still sees everything.

Weak points even with signing

Fake keys added to the trust list, a hijacked destination behind a valid signature, replay of a photographed code, and warnings people click through.

Now in the PoC: origin and path binding, checked against the final landing page. Next: server-side nonce store, key revocation, hard stop on UNVERIFIED for sensitive actions.
